🔤 Abbreviations & Glossary

All abbreviations used in this tool, listed alphabetically.

A
AGP
Ambulatory Glucose Profile — A standardised visual report of CGM data showing glucose patterns over a set period (typically 14 days). Used to assess TIR, TBR, and TAR at a glance.
AID
Automated Insulin Delivery — A system that automatically adjusts insulin delivery in response to CGM readings. Also called a closed-loop or artificial pancreas system.
AIT
Active Insulin Time — The duration over which the system considers a bolus dose to still be active in the body. Also called Duration of Insulin Action (DIA) or Insulin Duration. Shorter AIT = more aggressive correction behaviour.
B
BG
Blood Glucose — The concentration of glucose in the blood, measured in mmol/L. Used interchangeably with "blood sugar."
C
CF
Correction Factor — See ISF. How much 1 unit of insulin lowers blood glucose (mmol/L per unit). Used to calculate correction boluses.
CGM
Continuous Glucose Monitor — A wearable sensor that measures interstitial glucose every 1–5 minutes and transmits readings to a pump or app. The core input for all AID systems.
CR
Carbohydrate Ratio — See ICR. The grams of carbohydrate covered by 1 unit of insulin.
D
DIA
Duration of Insulin Action — See AIT. The period the system treats a bolus as still active. Typically 2–6 hours depending on the pump.
DSN
Diabetes Specialist Nurse — A nurse with specialist training in diabetes management, including insulin pump therapy and AID systems.
G
GMI
Glucose Management Indicator — An estimated HbA1c calculated from average CGM glucose over 14+ days. Provides a real-time surrogate for lab HbA1c.
H
HbA1c
Glycated Haemoglobin — A lab test reflecting average blood glucose over the preceding 2–3 months. Expressed as a percentage or mmol/mol. Target is typically <53 mmol/mol (<7%) for most adults with type 1 diabetes.
HCL
Hybrid Closed Loop — An AID system where the algorithm automates basal insulin but still requires user input for meal boluses (except iLet). "Hybrid" distinguishes it from a fully closed loop.
HCP
Healthcare Professional — A clinician involved in the patient's diabetes care, such as a DSN, consultant diabetologist, or GP.
I
ICR
Insulin:Carb Ratio — The grams of carbohydrate covered by 1 unit of bolus insulin. Expressed as "1:X" (e.g. 1:10 means 1 unit per 10 g carbs). A lower number = more insulin per gram = stronger ratio.
IOB
Insulin on Board — The estimated amount of active insulin remaining in the body from previous bolus and/or automated doses. AID systems track IOB to avoid insulin stacking and overcorrection.
ISF
Insulin Sensitivity Factor — How much 1 unit of insulin is expected to lower blood glucose (mmol/L per unit). Also called Correction Factor (CF). Used for correction bolus calculations.
M
mmol/L
Millimoles per Litre — The unit used in the UK and most of Europe to express blood glucose concentration. Normal fasting range is approximately 3.9–5.5 mmol/L.
P
PANTHER
Pump And Non-THErapy Research — A UK NHS programme providing structured education and clinical support for insulin pump and AID therapy. Source of device fact sheets used in this tool.
T
TAR
Time Above Range — The percentage of CGM readings above 10.0 mmol/L over a given period. International target: <25%. High TAR indicates persistent hyperglycaemia.
TBR
Time Below Range — The percentage of CGM readings below 3.9 mmol/L. International target: <4%. TBR ≥4% is a clinical priority — address hypoglycaemia before targeting hyperglycaemia.
TDD
Total Daily Dose — The total units of insulin delivered in 24 hours, including both basal and bolus insulin. Used to estimate ICR (500 ÷ TDD) and ISF (100 ÷ TDD in mmol/L). Typically split ~50% basal / 50% bolus.
TIR
Time in Range — The percentage of CGM readings between 3.9 and 10.0 mmol/L. International consensus target: >70% for most adults with type 1 diabetes on AID therapy.
